01 / Artwork

Learning by looking,
then looking again.

Five works move from observation and color into atmosphere, revision, and a final large-scale ecosystem. The car transformation makes the accumulated practice especially visible.

01

Learning value / observation

My Memory

Three ordinary possessions hold parts of an identity: a first high-end soccer cleat, a constantly carried water bottle, and headphones tied to music and solitude.

02

Learning color / mixed media

Potassium

A blueberry and french fries appear as opposites, yet share nutrients such as potassium. The piece reflects a period of becoming more conscious of food, fitness, and the body.

03

Atmosphere / narrative

Waiting to Be Found

An abandoned sports car is overtaken by the forest. Red paint cuts through the surrounding green, turning a familiar subject into a story about restoration and a possible second life.

04

The same page, revised

Red car transformation

Years later, I returned to the original drawing instead of starting over. In four focused hours, I strengthened the contrast, rebuilt the car and architecture, and added the specificity I could not achieve the first time.

International honorable mention
05

Learning composition / ecosystem

Beneath the Mangroves

The tangled roots shelter a sea turtle and tropical fish, turning apparent chaos into an interconnected habitat. The work received an Honorable Mention in the Mangrove Action Project’s international art contest.

02 / Identity

From making images
to building systems.

Two branding projects show a second kind of progression: first experimenting freely with a fictional label, then shaping a focused identity for a real company.

Project 01

Persona

Speculative identity / early experimentation

Persona began as a record and clothing concept. The work moves through sharp Y2K typography, distorted forms, glow, grain, and dramatically different color directions—an exercise in discovering how one mark can hold together across formats.

Project 02

Sunrise Systems

Company identity / final system

A real brief changed the goal from visual experimentation to usefulness. Two early directions led to a final Sunrise Systems logo, shown in its orange and white applications.

03 / About

The work changed.
The curiosity didn’t.

I’m Neel Das, an artist and builder who likes returning to unfinished ideas—reworking a drawing years later, studying how an ecosystem holds together, or turning an experimental mark into something a business can use.

The process stays consistent: start, notice what is missing, and return with better tools.
